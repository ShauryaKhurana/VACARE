VA compensation claims, in plain language

Five lanes, what each form actually is, and who physically fills it out. Core disability compensation only. Add-on benefits at the end.

Who fills it: Veteran Doctor Employer Witness VA Hard rule

The four documents everyone mixes up

They all sound like "a statement about my claim." They are four different things.

Statement in Support of Claim · 21-4138 · Veteran

A blank page with your name on it. The veteran writes anything in their own words: how the knee actually feels, why the dates in the record are wrong, what daily life looks like. No medical weight. This is not the nexus letter.

Buddy statement · 21-10210 · Witness

Same idea, different author. A spouse, squadmate, or supervisor writes what they saw and signs it themselves. "I served with him in 2019 and watched the accident happen." Firsthand observation only.

Nexus letter · no form number exists · Doctor

A letter on a doctor's letterhead connecting the condition to service, using the magic words "at least as likely as not." This is the link between diagnosis and service, and in a contested claim it's the most valuable page in the file. VA never hands you a form for it because VA doesn't ask for it; you bring it.

DBQ (Disability Benefits Questionnaire) · 21-0960 series · Doctor

A checkbox exam form, one version per condition (~70 of them). A doctor scores your symptoms against VA's exact rating criteria. It measures how bad, while the nexus letter argues why it's service-connected. Different jobs.

C&P exam · not a form at all · VA

The exam VA schedules with its own examiner after you file. You attend, they write it up. Skipping it without good cause sinks the claim.

LANE 1

"I'm getting out"

Filing before separation so the rating decision lands right around discharge day. The claim form is the same one used everywhere else; the timing is what's special.

  1. Check the window 180–90 days out

    BDD only exists between 180 and 90 days before separation. Day 89? Same paperwork, slower track. In a medical board (MEB/IDES)? Different lane entirely — the claim rides on 21-0819 and DoD runs the exams.

  2. Hire your rep · 21-22 Veteran + VSO signs

    Legal permission for a Veterans Service Officer to file for you and see your file. Free. Skip if going alone.

  3. File the claim · 21-526EZ Veteran

    The master application: every condition being claimed, listed on one form. For BDD it must go in with complete service treatment records attached, including anything a Guard or Reserve unit is sitting on. Incomplete records = ejected from BDD.

  4. Stay available for exams 45 days

    VA schedules the C&P exams before separation. Unreachable or on terminal leave somewhere else = ejected from BDD.

  5. Separate; DD-214 closes the loop VA

    Rating decision targets the days right after discharge. Pay starts from day one of civilian life.

If any claimed condition is mental health: add 21-0781, the stressor statement — the veteran describes the event(s) behind it so VA can verify them in unit records.
LANE 2

"I've never filed"

The first claim after service. The work is proving three things: a current diagnosis, something that happened in service, and a link between the two.

  1. Raise your hand · 21-0966 Intent to File Veteran

    One page, no evidence, no conditions listed. It locks today's date so that when the claim is approved months later, back pay runs from now. Good for 12 months. Costs nothing; skipping it costs money.

  2. Hire your rep · 21-22 Veteran + VSO signs

    Or 21-22a for a private attorney or claims agent. One rep at a time — filing a new one silently fires the old one.

  3. Unlock private records · 21-4142 + 21-4142a Veteran

    The permission slip that lets VA pull records straight from civilian doctors and hospitals. The 4142 is the signature; the 4142a is the list of who to call. The signature expires after 12 months — long claims may need a fresh one.

  4. File the claim · 21-526EZ Veteran

    List every condition. The form also asks a question worth understanding: "Fully Developed Claim" means "I've attached everything, don't go looking" — faster if true, quietly downgraded to standard if VA has to fetch anything.

  5. Show up to the C&P exam No-show = decided without you

    VA's examiner, VA's schedule. On a first claim a miss means they decide on whatever paper exists.

Mental health condition: add 21-0781 (veteran). Someone saw what happened: add a buddy statement, 21-10210 (the witness fills and signs it, not the veteran). Anything that needs saying in plain words: 21-4138 (veteran).
LANE 3

"I got worse"

Already rated for a condition; asking for a higher percentage because it deteriorated. Shortest paperwork stack of all the lanes — the fight is entirely about evidence of severity.

  1. Gather proof it's worse Veteran Doctor

    Recent treatment records, and ideally a DBQ where a doctor scores the condition against VA's own criteria. "Trust me, it's worse" loses; measurements win. Private records need a fresh 21-4142.

  2. File the claim · 21-526EZ Veteran

    Same master form, marked as a claim for increase on the listed condition.

  3. Show up to the C&P exam No-show on an increase = denial

    Harsher rule than first claims: missing this one can end it outright.

Fair warning to build into UX: asking for an increase reopens the rating for review. Ratings held under 5 years have little protection against being lowered instead.
LANE 4

"Something new came up"

A condition not yet on the rating — either standalone, or caused by one that is (sleep apnea from the rated rhinitis, depression from the chronic pain). Veterans rarely know the "caused by" version exists, and it's often the easier win.

  1. Get the connecting opinion — the nexus letter Doctor

    No VA form. A doctor writes, on letterhead, that the new condition is "at least as likely as not" caused or aggravated by service or by the already-rated condition. For a secondary claim this letter basically is the claim.

  2. Unlock the records · 21-4142 + 21-4142a Veteran

    Same permission slip as Lane 2, covering the doctors treating the new condition.

  3. File the claim · 21-526EZ Veteran

    New condition listed; for secondary, named alongside the service-connected condition causing it.

  4. Show up to the C&P exam Same no-show rule

LANE 5

"They got it wrong"

Denied, or rated lower than deserved. One decision to make, three doors, and the right door depends on a single question: is there evidence VA hasn't seen? Every clock starts on the decision letter's date.

20-0995 · Supplemental Claim Veteran
FILE WITHIN 1 YEAR TO KEEP THE ORIGINAL PAY DATE

"Here's something you haven't seen." New evidence goes in — a nexus letter, new records, a buddy statement — and a rater decides again. The default door: most disagreements have at least one new piece of paper. Filing late is allowed but resets the back-pay clock.

20-0996 · Higher-Level Review Veteran
HARD 1-YEAR DEADLINE · NO NEW EVIDENCE ACCEPTED

"Same file, look again — you made a mistake." A senior rater rereads exactly what's already there. Nothing new may be added, so this only works when the error is visible in the existing record.

10182 · Board Appeal Veteran
HARD 1-YEAR DEADLINE · PICK A DOCKET ON THE FORM

"I want a judge." A Veterans Law Judge decides. The form itself asks which flavor: judge reads the file as-is (fastest), 90 days to add evidence, or a hearing. Slowest door, strongest review. Can't take this door twice in a row.

Decision dated before Feb 19, 2019 and still grinding through the old system: the legacy pair is 21-0958 then VA Form 9. Shrinking population, not zero.

Add-ons

Extra money or benefits stacked onto a compensation claim. Each rides on the 21-526EZ or follows a rating; each has its own form and, often, its own author.

"My conditions keep me from holding a job" — TDIU (paid at the 100% rate)
21-8940 Veteran  +  21-4192 Employer

The 8940 is the veteran's work history and why work is impossible. The 4192 is the employer's side — last employer(s) confirm dates, duties, why it ended. VA won't grant unemployability without the 8940, even when the file screams it. After a grant, 21-4140 arrives annually to confirm still-not-working; ignoring it triggers a reduction.

"I need help with daily living, or can't leave the house" — Aid & Attendance
21-2680 Doctor  ·  nursing home: 21-0779 Facility

The veteran cannot self-complete either one. The 2680 is an exam a physician performs and signs; the 0779 is filled out by the nursing home's office. The product's job here is routing paper to the right third party and chasing it.

"I have a spouse / kids / dependent parent" — extra monthly pay at 30%+
21-686c Veteran  ·  student child 18–23: 21-674 Veteran

Only pays once the combined rating hits 30%. The money rule: filed within 1 year of the decision that crossed 30%, the extra pay is retroactive to the rating date. Filed later, it only runs from filing day. VA occasionally sends 21-0538 to reverify.

"I need my home adapted" — SAH/SHA housing grant
26-4555 Veteran

For specific severe service-connected losses (limbs, blindness, certain burns). Comes after the qualifying rating exists, not with the claim.

"I need an adapted vehicle" — auto allowance + adaptive equipment
21-4502 Veteran + VA certifies  ·  equipment only: 10-1394 Veteran

One-time payment toward a vehicle for qualifying losses. Buy the car before VA approves and the money is gone — approval first, purchase second, no exceptions. Adaptive equipment (10-1394) is separate and repeatable.

"My prosthetic/brace/skin meds destroy my clothes" — annual clothing allowance
10-8678 Veteran

Small yearly payment. Deadline is August 1, every year. Miss it, wait twelve months.

"I just had surgery / a long hospitalization for a rated condition" — temporary 100%
no dedicated form — 21-4138 Veteran + hospital records

Full rate during recovery: 21+ days hospitalized, or convalescence after surgery. Claim within a year of the event for full back pay. Constantly missed because nothing prompts it.

"I'm in the Guard/Reserve and drilled this year"
21-8951-2 Veteran

Can't collect drill pay and disability pay for the same days; this annual form waives one for the other. Housekeeping, but ignoring it creates a debt letter.
